\section{Introduction}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Financial sentiment analysis on social media has practical applications in algorithmic trading, market surveillance, and investor sentiment tracking.
Unlike general-purpose sentiment analysis, financial tweets present unique challenges: domain-specific vocabulary (cashtags, financial jargon), extreme brevity, and inherent class imbalance where neutral commentary dominates.

We frame the problem as multi-class text classification: given a financial tweet, predict whether it expresses \textbf{Bearish}~(0), \textbf{Bullish}~(1), or \textbf{Neutral}~(2) sentiment.
Our approach systematically evaluates \textbf{32~combinations} of feature extraction methods and classifiers using 5-fold stratified cross-validation, with macro-F1 as the primary metric.
Each step builds on the limitations of the previous one.

The best pipeline --- \textbf{Twitter-RoBERTa embeddings + XGBoost} --- achieves a macro-F1 of \textbf{0.794}, significantly outperforming traditional approaches and demonstrating that domain-specific pretrained representations are more important than classifier sophistication.

\section{Data Exploration}
\label{sec:data}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The dataset consists of \textbf{9,543~training tweets} and \textbf{2,388~test tweets}, each labeled with one of three sentiment classes.
The dataset exhibits significant class imbalance --- Neutral tweets (64.7\%) outnumber Bearish tweets (15.1\%) by a factor of 4.3$\times$ (Table~\ref{tab:class_dist}).
This motivates two key design decisions: (1)~using \texttt{class\_weight='balanced'} in all classifiers, and (2)~adopting macro-F1 rather than accuracy as the primary metric, since a naive all-Neutral classifier would achieve 64.7\% accuracy while failing entirely on minority classes.

\section{Data Preprocessing}
\label{sec:preprocessing}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

We implemented \textbf{composable preprocessing functions} in \texttt{src/preprocessing.py}, allowing us to mix and match cleaning steps into named pipelines and compare them systematically.

\subsection{Preprocessing Functions}

Each cleaning step is a standalone function:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{HTML unescape} --- Decode \texttt{\&amp;}, \texttt{\&lt;}, etc.
    \item \textbf{URL removal} --- Strip \texttt{http://} and \texttt{www.} links.
    \item \textbf{Mention removal} --- Strip \texttt{@username} handles.
    \item \textbf{Hashtag handling} --- Strip \texttt{\#} but keep the word.
    \item \textbf{Ticker normalization} --- Replace cashtags with \texttt{<TICKER>}.
    \item \textbf{Number normalization} --- Percentages $\rightarrow$ \texttt{<PCT>}, numbers $\rightarrow$ \texttt{<NUM>}.
    \item \textbf{Emoji demojization} --- Convert emojis to text descriptions, preserving sentiment signal.
    \item \textbf{Unicode normalization} --- NFKC for consistent encoding.
    \item \textbf{Lowercasing} --- Standard case folding.
    \item \textbf{Punctuation collapsing} --- \texttt{!!!} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{!}, whitespace normalization.
    \item \textbf{Financial-aware stopword removal} --- We keep negations (\texttt{no}, \texttt{not}, \texttt{nor}) and directional words (\texttt{up}, \texttt{down}, \texttt{over}, \texttt{under}) which are critical for financial sentiment.
    \item \textbf{Lemmatization/Stemming} --- Tested as optional token-level operations.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Feature Engineering}
\label{sec:features}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

We implement and compare \textbf{eight feature extraction methods} across three families.

\subsection{Bag-of-Words Features}

\textbf{CountVectorizer (BoW)}: Unigram counts with a 10,000-feature vocabulary cap.
\textbf{TF-IDF}: Bigrams (\texttt{ngram\_range=(1,2)}), sublinear TF scaling, and 20,000~features with \texttt{min\_df=2} and \texttt{max\_df=0.95}.
TF-IDF outperforms BoW by 1--3~percentage points across all classifiers, confirming that local word order carries sentiment signal (Figure~\ref{fig:tfidf}).

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.82\textwidth]{top_tfidf_features.png}
\caption{Top TF-IDF features per class, extracted from Logistic Regression coefficients.}
\label{fig:tfidf}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Word Embeddings}

\textbf{Custom Word2Vec}: Skip-gram, 100d, trained on the financial tweet corpus with mean pooling.
\textbf{GloVe Twitter 100d}: Pretrained on 2~billion tweets.
\textbf{TF-IDF-Weighted Pooling}: Weights each token embedding by its TF-IDF score.

All Word2Vec variants perform significantly worse than BoW/TF-IDF (macro-F1: 0.59--0.66 vs.\ 0.72--0.74).
The primary reason is that \textbf{mean pooling loses word order}, which is critical for sentiment --- ``stock going up'' and ``stock going down'' produce similar mean vectors.

\subsection{Transformer Encoders (Mandatory + Extra Work)}

We use pretrained Transformer encoders as \textbf{frozen feature extractors}: tokenize, pass through the frozen encoder, mean-pool the last hidden states (768-dim) with attention masking, and feed the result to a downstream sklearn classifier.
Embeddings are cached as \texttt{.npy} files, making this CPU-feasible.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Transformer encoders evaluated as frozen feature extractors.}
\label{tab:transformers}
\begin{tabular}{llrl}
\toprule
\textbf{Encoder} & \textbf{Domain} & \textbf{Params} & \textbf{Status} \\
\midrule
DistilBERT & General & 66M & Mandatory \\
FinBERT & Finance (SEC filings) & 110M & Extra work \\
Twitter-RoBERTa & Twitter + sentiment & 125M & Extra work \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\textbf{Key finding}: Domain alignment is more important than model size.
DistilBERT (general-purpose, 66M~params) underperforms TF-IDF, while Twitter-RoBERTa (tweet-domain, sentiment-tuned, 125M~params) achieves the best results overall.
FinBERT falls between --- it understands financial language but was trained on formal SEC filings, not informal tweets.

\subsection{Key Findings}

\textbf{Feature hierarchy} (most to least effective):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Twitter-RoBERTa} (0.794) --- pretrained on 58M tweets with sentiment labels; perfect domain alignment.
    \item \textbf{FinBERT} (0.779) --- financial domain knowledge helps, but trained on formal text.
    \item \textbf{TF-IDF} (0.740) --- surprisingly competitive; bigrams capture local word order.
    \item \textbf{BoW} (0.720) --- solid baseline without bigram advantage.
    \item \textbf{DistilBERT} (0.705) --- general-purpose encoder \textit{underperforms} TF-IDF, demonstrating that domain alignment matters more than model sophistication.
    \item \textbf{Word2Vec variants} (0.658) --- mean pooling destroys word order.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Classifier patterns}: XGBoost excels with dense Transformer embeddings (non-linear boundaries in 768-dim space), while linear models (LogReg, SVM) excel with sparse features (BoW/TF-IDF) where regularization handles high dimensionality well.
Random Forest consistently underperforms both.

All standard deviations are below 0.017, and the best model has one of the lowest ($\pm$0.006), indicating stable, reproducible results.

Neutral is consistently the easiest class (F1~$>$~0.87) due to large support and distinctive factual vocabulary.
Bearish and Bullish are harder (F1: 0.65--0.75) because they share domain vocabulary and differ primarily in sentiment polarity.
Bearish is slightly harder --- bearish tweets sometimes use hedging language (``might drop'') that resembles neutral commentary.

\subsection{Error Analysis}

Examining misclassified tweets reveals three main failure patterns:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Neutral $\leftrightarrow$ Bearish}: Tweets reporting negative news factually (e.g., ``Stock down 5\% after earnings miss'') are genuinely ambiguous between factual reporting and negative sentiment.
    \item \textbf{Neutral $\leftrightarrow$ Bullish}: Factual reports of positive events blur the line between informational and positive sentiment.
    \item \textbf{Bearish $\leftrightarrow$ Bullish} (rarest): Primarily sarcasm or contrarian positions.
\end{enumerate}

The primary challenge is the \textbf{fundamental ambiguity between factual reporting and sentiment expression} in financial discourse.

\section{EXTRA WORK}
\label{sec:extra}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{EXTRA WORK --- Domain-Specific Transformer Encoders}

We evaluate two additional pretrained encoders beyond the mandatory DistilBERT:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{FinBERT} (\texttt{ProsusAI/finbert}): Trained on financial communications.
    Achieves 0.779~macro-F1, a \textbf{7.4~pp improvement} over DistilBERT.
    \item \textbf{Twitter-RoBERTa} (\texttt{cardiffnlp/twitter-roberta-base-sentiment\--latest}): Trained on 58M~tweets with sentiment labels.
    Achieves \textbf{0.794~macro-F1}, the best result overall.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{EXTRA WORK --- Zero-Shot Pretrained Classification}
\label{sec:zero_shot}

To isolate the contribution of the trained downstream classifier from that of the pretrained encoder, we apply Twitter-RoBERTa's built-in 3-class sentiment head (negative/neutral/positive) directly to our validation set --- no training of our own.
Outputs are mapped to our label space: negative\,$\rightarrow$\,Bearish, positive\,$\rightarrow$\,Bullish, neutral\,$\rightarrow$\,Neutral.

The zero-shot baseline reaches \textbf{0.587~macro-F1} (5-fold aggregation: $0.587 \pm 0.012$), well below the 0.794 obtained when the same encoder is paired with a trained XGBoost classifier (Table~\ref{tab:zero_shot}).

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Same encoder, two heads: zero-shot vs.\ supervised adaptation.}
\label{tab:zero_shot}
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
\textbf{Approach (Twitter-RoBERTa encoder)} & \textbf{Macro-F1} \\
\midrule
Zero-shot (pretrained 3-class head, no training) & 0.587 \\
Frozen embeddings + XGBoost (ours)               & \textbf{0.794} \\
\midrule
$\Delta$ from supervised adaptation              & \textbf{+0.207} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The gap concentrates in the minority classes --- Bearish/Bullish F1 fall from 0.65--0.75 (trained) to 0.48--0.49 (zero-shot), while Neutral degrades less.
This reflects the misalignment between the model's pretraining objective (general Twitter sentiment) and the financial-sentiment label space: a tweet using ``downgrade'', ``cut'' or ``miss'' is bearish in finance but not unambiguously negative in everyday speech.
Supervised adaptation on $\sim$9.5k labeled examples is what recovers the missing $+0.21$~macro-F1; pretraining domain alignment is necessary but not sufficient.

\subsection{EXTRA WORK --- Agentic Workflow}

We implement a LangChain-based agent (\texttt{src/agent.py}) with five tools: \textit{classify\_tweet}, \textit{route\_model} (intelligent routing based on tweet characteristics), \textit{ensemble\_classify} (majority-vote across all models), \textit{compare\_models}, and \textit{explain\_\allowbreak prediction} (coefficient-based feature attribution).

The agent demonstrates non-trivial decision-making: it inspects tweet content to choose the most appropriate model (e.g., cashtags $\rightarrow$ FinBERT), reconciles model disagreements via ensemble voting, and provides human-interpretable explanations.
Three example transcripts illustrate these capabilities:

\textbf{Transcript~1 --- Model Routing.}
Input: \textit{``\$AAPL just hit all-time high, massive breakout above resistance.''}
The agent detects a \$TICKER cashtag and routes to FinBERT.
Result: \textbf{Bullish} (89.1\% confidence). The routing decision demonstrates domain-aware model selection.

\textbf{Transcript~2 --- Ensemble with Disagreement.}
Input: \textit{``Markets mixed today as Fed signals possible rate changes amid inflation concerns.''}
The agent runs all four models: TF-IDF/LogReg, FinBERT, and Twitter-RoBERTa predict Neutral; DistilBERT predicts Bearish.
Result: \textbf{Neutral} (75\% agreement). DistilBERT's error illustrates why domain alignment matters --- it over-weights ``concerns'' and ``inflation'' without financial context.

\textbf{Transcript~3 --- Feature Attribution.}
Input: \textit{``Sell everything, this stock is going to crash hard.''}
The agent uses TF-IDF + LogReg coefficients to explain: ``sell'' (+2.85) and ``crash'' (+2.63) are the strongest Bearish signals; both have strong negative coefficients for Bullish ($-$1.92, $-$2.10), actively pushing the prediction away from that class.

\subsection{EXTRA WORK --- Decoder Model (Attempted)}

We implemented few-shot classification using Flan-T5-small with 2~examples per class.
CPU inference proved infeasible (${\sim}$2--5s per tweet $\times$ 2,388~tweets $>$ 3~hours), documented as a limitation.

\section{Final Pipeline and Predictions}
\label{sec:final}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The production pipeline (\texttt{tm\_final\_42.ipynb}) trains the best model on the \textbf{full training set} (9,543~tweets) and generates predictions for all 2,388~test tweets:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Load and preprocess all data with \texttt{pp\_transformer}.
    \item Extract Twitter-RoBERTa embeddings (cached as \texttt{.npy} files).
    \item Train XGBoost on all 9,543~training embeddings.
    \item Predict on 2,388~test tweets $\rightarrow$ \texttt{pred\_42.csv}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Our systematic evaluation of 32~feature $\times$ classifier combinations reveals that domain-specific pretrained Transformer encoders combined with gradient-boosted classifiers achieve the best results.
The winning pipeline --- Twitter-RoBERTa + XGBoost --- achieves \textbf{0.794~macro-F1}.

\textbf{Key takeaways}:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Domain alignment $>$ model sophistication}: DistilBERT (66M~params) underperforms TF-IDF bigrams; Twitter-RoBERTa (125M~params) exceeds both.
    \textit{What} a model was trained on matters more than \textit{how large} it is.
    Alignment alone is not enough either --- the same encoder applied zero-shot scores 0.587 (Section~\ref{sec:zero_shot}); the trained head adds $+0.21$~macro-F1.

    \item \textbf{Feature extraction matters more than classifier choice}: The gap between feature families (0.60 for W2V vs.\ 0.79 for Twitter-RoBERTa) far exceeds the gap between classifiers within a family (0.02--0.05).

    \item \textbf{Conservative preprocessing wins}: Aggressive normalization destroys sentiment signal.
    Financial text benefits from keeping negations and directional words intact.

    \item \textbf{Class imbalance handling is essential}: Without balanced class weights, classifiers default to predicting Neutral.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Limitations}: CPU-only constraint prevented full fine-tuning; no hyperparameter optimization; single dataset; decoder model infeasible on CPU.

\textbf{Future work}: Full fine-tuning with GPU; ensemble methods across feature families; temporal analysis of financial sentiment patterns; Bayesian hyperparameter optimization.
